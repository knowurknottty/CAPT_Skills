# Source and Claim Matrix

## Source choice
Choose sources by what the claim requires, not by prestige alone.
- Original/primary: statutes, court records, official datasets, source code, standards, patents, first-party technical docs, original papers/data, archival records, direct measurements.
- Secondary synthesis: systematic reviews, high-quality journalism, expert histories, technical analyses. Use for context, discovery, comparison, and fields where many primary records must be synthesized.
- Discovery-only: search snippets, aggregators, unsourced summaries, model output. Follow these to evidence before relying on the claim.

## Claim states
- `VERIFIED`: directly supported by an appropriate authoritative/original source or direct observation.
- `CORROBORATED`: supported consistently by multiple independent credible sources when direct primary evidence is unavailable or the claim is synthetic.
- `INFERRED`: reasoned conclusion from cited evidence; show the inference chain.
- `CONTESTED`: credible sources materially disagree; preserve the disagreement and likely reasons.
- `UNVERIFIED`: insufficient evidence, inaccessible load-bearing source, or unsupported inherited assertion.

## Saturation check per thread
A thread is dispositioned when it has: a clear subquestion; evidence appropriate to that claim type; source identity/date/version; at least one deliberate contradiction/alternative search; any inference shown separately; and an explicit conclusion or gap. More search is justified when it changes the claim state, exposes a new material alternative, or closes a known gap—not merely when it adds citations saying the same thing.
